# ART Falsification Program

The falsification program is the core of this repository.

ART should not accumulate supportive examples without equal effort to find cases that force revision.

## What counts as a useful contribution

High-value contributions include:

- a real system that works without reconstructing a claimed inevitable structure;
- a simpler ontology that preserves the same information and predictions;
- an event that cannot be naturally represented by the current model;
- a domain where the proposed structure creates artificial complexity rather than compression;
- a case where two supposedly distinct ART concepts collapse without information loss;
- independent designs that converge on a materially different grammar;
- empirical evidence that verification economics make the proposed infrastructure non-viable.

## Test sequence

For each candidate structure:

```text
1. Define the operational setting.
2. Remove the structure.
3. Attempt to keep the system functional.
4. Permit all reasonable alternatives.
5. Observe whether an equivalent semantics reappears.
6. Compare information loss and implementation cost.
7. Repeat across unrelated domains.
8. Publish counterexamples before expanding the theory.
```

## Candidate structures under active attack

| Candidate | Current status | Strongest open attack |
|---|---|---|
| Target | strong candidate | terminal-state-only systems may not need explicit baseline/transition semantics |
| Bounded Authority | strong candidate | capability systems may absorb many separate authority concepts |
| Verification | strong candidate | trusted or deterministic environments may not require an independent verification layer |
| Judgment | conditional candidate | deterministic policy execution may eliminate explicit Judgment |
| Delegation | conditional candidate | capability transfer may provide a simpler representation |
| Termination | semantics strong; event form unproven | expiry/state invalidation may remove the need for first-class events |
| Causal Attribution | downstream candidate | not required for basic operation |
| Responsibility | institutional function | may remain external to protocol semantics |
| Settlement | economic consequence | existing payment/contract layers may be sufficient |

## Anti-confirmation rule

A case is not evidence for ART merely because it can be mapped to ART terminology. Mapping flexibility can hide overfitting.

Evidence becomes stronger when:

- the mapping is forced by operational constraints;
- deleting a structure causes a measurable failure;
- independent systems reconstruct an isomorphic structure;
- the model predicts a later engineering requirement before it becomes common practice.

## Counterexample policy

Counterexamples are first-class research outputs. They should not be deleted when the theory changes. Historical failed hypotheses should remain visible.
