# ART Prediction Ledger

This ledger records predictions **before** the relevant outcomes are known. Predictions should not be rewritten after the fact. Later commentary may be appended with dates.

**Initial ledger date:** 2026-08-15

## P-01 — Judgment provenance becomes explicit

**Prediction:** As autonomous systems gain greater discretion and authority, execution provenance and authorization records will increasingly be judged insufficient for high-consequence decisions. Systems will add explicit records of which actor accepted, selected, rejected, approved, ranked, or otherwise committed to the consequential proposition that led to action.

**Falsifier:** mature high-consequence autonomous systems operate at scale with no demand for decision/judgment commitment provenance beyond ordinary policy and action logs.

## P-02 — Termination semantics become first-class in agent authority systems

**Prediction:** Long-lived agents and multi-hop delegation will force revocation, expiry, completion, supersession, and related closure semantics to become explicit interoperability concerns.

**Falsifier:** authority systems remain safely interoperable at scale without portable lifecycle/closure semantics.

## P-03 — Action receipts expand toward outcome evidence

**Prediction:** Receipts that prove only that an action was executed will be insufficient for many commercial and institutional use cases. Systems will increasingly represent postconditions, observed state changes, or outcome evidence.

**Falsifier:** execution receipts alone remain sufficient for automated settlement and accountability in outcome-sensitive markets.

## P-04 — Cross-organizational responsibility/provenance graphs emerge

**Prediction:** Multi-organization agent workflows will create demand for portable graphs connecting principal, judgment/intent, delegation, authority, execution, verification, and downstream consequence.

**Falsifier:** organization-local logs remain sufficient even when agents routinely cross organizational boundaries.

## P-05 — Verification becomes a larger share of transaction cost

**Prediction:** As autonomous execution becomes cheaper, the relative economic importance of reliable verification, measurement, evidence qualification, and dispute handling will increase in domains where outcomes matter.

**Falsifier:** execution costs fall while verification remains economically negligible or unnecessary across the same domains.

## P-06 — Verified-transition contracting grows selectively

**Prediction:** In domains with high observability, clear acceptance conditions, and verification cost much lower than outcome value, some transactions will shift from paying for labor/time toward paying for verified state transitions or milestones.

**Falsifier:** even in high-observability/low-verification-cost domains, contracting remains persistently tied to effort rather than verified outcomes.

## P-07 — Agent infrastructure pressure moves upward

**Prediction:** After identity, authentication, payment, and tool interoperability mature, a growing share of unresolved problems will move toward target semantics, bounded authority, judgment provenance, verification, attribution, and responsibility evidence.

**Falsifier:** mature agent ecosystems solve cross-trust action without increasing demand for these higher-order structures.

## P-08 — Independent systems converge on target/authority/verification equivalents

**Prediction:** Teams designing consequential autonomous systems independently will repeatedly introduce semantic equivalents of Target, Bounded Authority, and Verification even when not exposed to ART vocabulary.

**Falsifier:** mature independent designs systematically avoid one or more of these structures without reconstructing equivalents.
