# ART v0.1 — Accountable Reality Transition

**Status:** Conceptual preprint / falsifiable research hypothesis  
**Publisher:** Cognitive Emergence  
**Date:** 2026-08-15

## 1. Problem statement

Autonomous systems increasingly move from producing information to causing consequential changes in digital, economic, organizational, and physical environments. Once action crosses a trust boundary, ordinary execution logs are insufficient to answer the full set of questions surrounding a change.

ART asks whether these apparently separate questions are projections of a more stable underlying structure.

## 2. Working model

Let a relevant observed state be represented as `S_t^P`, where `P` is a domain-specific projection rather than a claim to represent all of reality. A transition is:

```text
τ : S_t^P → S_(t+1)^P'
```

ART studies transitions that involve consequential actor participation and for which control, evidence, or institutional consequence matters.

A descriptive research tuple is:

```text
ART = <S0, G, J, Au, X, T, V, C, R, Σ, S1>
```

where:

- `S0` — relevant initial observed state;
- `G` — goal / target conditions;
- `J` — judgment or commitment associated with selecting/accepting a consequential proposition;
- `Au` — bounded authority, including scope and constraints;
- `X` — execution/action trajectory;
- `T` — termination/lifecycle closure semantics;
- `V` — evidence-based verification/determination;
- `C` — causal attribution;
- `R` — normative responsibility assignment;
- `Σ` — settlement or consequence allocation;
- `S1` — resulting observed/accepted state.

The tuple is a research map, not a claim that every implementation must serialize every component.

## 3. Strongest current inevitability candidates

### Target

Purposeful action requires an answer to what change is intended and what conditions count as acceptable completion. If a system removes explicit target semantics, an equivalent tends to reappear as goals, postconditions, acceptance criteria, or desired state.

### Bounded Authority

When an actor uses resources, identities, data, accounts, devices, or powers controlled by another actor, the system requires a boundary around what may be changed. A bare boolean authorization is generally insufficient; scope, constraints, and validity reappear.

### Verification

Across trust boundaries, a claim that a transition occurred cannot generally be treated as self-authenticating. Evidence, measurement, attestation, observation, audit, or an equivalent determination mechanism reappears.

These claims remain falsifiable and conditional on the stated problem setting.

## 4. Conditional structures

- **Judgment** becomes important when multiple consequential futures are possible and an actor commits to selecting, accepting, rejecting, ranking, or otherwise fixing one proposition.
- **Delegation** becomes necessary when bounded authority propagates across actors.
- **Termination** semantics become necessary when authority or commitment relationships have a lifecycle, although a first-class termination *event* is not yet proven to be necessary.
- **Causal Attribution** becomes important for learning, optimization, liability analysis, insurance, or contribution allocation.
- **Responsibility** is an institutional function; ART does not assume that technical provenance mechanically determines legal or moral responsibility.
- **Settlement** becomes important when accepted transitions trigger economic or institutional consequences.

## 5. Key separations

ART currently treats the following distinctions as important hypotheses:

```text
Intelligence ≠ Judgment
Judgment ≠ Authorization
Action ≠ Outcome
Verification ≠ Truth
Causality ≠ Responsibility
Target specification ≠ actual verification event
```

Each separation must be tested for reducibility and practical information gain.

## 6. Non-claims

ART v0.1 does not claim:

1. that state-transition theory is new;
2. that all action requires an explicit Judgment event;
3. that J/D/T/V is already a proven minimal primitive set;
4. that cryptographic evidence establishes external truth;
5. that technical provenance automatically establishes legal liability;
6. that all markets will become outcome markets;
7. that TSTO or JEP is the unique engineering realization of ART;
8. that ART is a universal law.

## 7. Falsification criteria

ART should be weakened or rejected if one or more of the following persists under serious testing:

- systems can cross trust boundaries and achieve accountable autonomous action without reconstructing target, authority, or verification equivalents;
- a strictly simpler existing model captures the same information and predictions;
- the proposed structure fails to recur outside AI-agent examples;
- independently designed systems do not converge toward semantically similar components;
- ART provides no predictive advantage over existing security, workflow, provenance, state-machine, and institutional models.

## 8. Engineering relation

ART is intentionally above specific specifications. TSTO is a candidate object representation for intended/acceptable state transition conditions. JEP is a candidate event representation for judgment-related control events. Their success or failure must be evaluated independently of ART.

## 9. Research program

The next stage is not broader advocacy. It is:

```text
definition freeze
→ adversarial corpus
→ deletion/substitution tests
→ alternative grammars
→ timestamped predictions
→ independent reproduction
→ real-world implementations
```

The project should prefer a smaller surviving theory over a larger defended one.
