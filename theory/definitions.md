# Working Definitions

These definitions are provisional. They exist to make claims attackable and comparable.

## Reality State

A **reality state** is not a claim to encode the complete world. ART uses a domain-relevant, evidence-linked projection of reality at a given time.

## Reality Transition

A **reality transition** is a change from one relevant state projection to another.

## Target

A **Target** is a machine- or human-evaluable description of the state conditions that an action is intended to bring about, including completion conditions when needed.

## Constraint

A **Constraint** is a condition that must remain satisfied during or at the completion of a transition. A target without constraints may permit unacceptable paths to completion.

## Bounded Authority

**Bounded Authority** is the permission or power of an actor to cause a specified class of transitions within defined scope, constraints, validity, and other applicable limits.

## Judgment

A **Judgment** is a consequential commitment by an actor that selects, accepts, rejects, ranks, classifies, approves, or otherwise fixes a proposition within a space of possible transitions or claims.

This definition intentionally distinguishes Judgment from raw intelligence, prediction, recommendation, or unconstrained internal reasoning.

## Delegation

**Delegation** is the propagation of bounded authority or a defined responsibility-bearing role from one actor to another under constraints.

## Action / Execution

An **Action** is an actor-caused operation that may contribute to a reality transition. Successful execution does not imply successful outcome.

## Termination

**Termination semantics** define when an authority, delegation, commitment, or related control relationship ceases to apply, including revocation, expiry, completion, replacement, cancellation, or exhaustion.

A first-class Termination event is a candidate engineering primitive, not yet a theoretical inevitability.

## Evidence

**Evidence** is an observation, record, receipt, measurement, attestation, snapshot, proof, or other material used by a verifier to evaluate a claim.

## Verification

**Verification** is an evidence-based determination about whether a specified claim or transition condition is satisfied under a defined verification context or policy.

Verification is not identical to objective truth.

## Outcome

An **Outcome** is the resulting state or accepted result of a transition. ART currently treats Outcome as a derived concept rather than an independent primitive candidate.

## Causal Attribution

**Causal Attribution** concerns what actors, actions, events, or conditions contributed to an observed transition.

## Responsibility

**Responsibility** is a normative assignment of consequences or obligations under legal, organizational, contractual, social, or other institutional rules.

Responsibility is not mechanically equivalent to causality.

## Settlement

**Settlement** is the redistribution of value, rights, permissions, reputation, penalties, insurance claims, liabilities, or other institutional consequences after an accepted transition or determination.

## Trust Boundary

A **trust boundary** exists where one actor or system cannot safely treat another actor's claims, authority, execution, or evidence as self-authenticating without additional controls.

## Structural Inevitability

A structure is a candidate for **structural inevitability** within a stated problem domain if removing its explicit representation causes a semantically equivalent structure to be reconstructed elsewhere in order for the system to continue satisfying its requirements.

## Independent Convergence

**Independent convergence** occurs when designers who are not given the ART vocabulary and do not coordinate on its model nevertheless derive semantically isomorphic structures under comparable constraints.
